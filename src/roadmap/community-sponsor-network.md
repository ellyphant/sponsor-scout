A useful introduction should not stop at the edge of one operator's event list. Build an opt-in network where independent communities can find complementary opportunities while retaining control of their relationships.

## What it looks like

- A host authorizes a time-limited event-needs brief; a sponsor can separately choose to publish the audiences, regions and opportunities it wants to hear about.
- Participating desks discover compatible briefs and see the evidence or explicit preferences behind each proposed connection.
- An operator requests an introduction through the responsible desk, rather than acquiring a private email list.
- Willing participants move into a permissioned introduction room with an agreed next step.

## Key details

- Requires independent event sources and Introduction Rooms. This is a future expansion beyond Elly's private desk, not an initial-build marketplace or a change to CoHost Club.
- Publishing requires the affected host's or sponsor's specific permission. Reading a CoHost response does not grant republication rights.
- Shared needs carry expiry, source identity and an authoritative event link. Withdrawn or stale briefs stop generating new proposed introductions.
- Keep personal contacts, internal qualification scores, private evidence, correspondence and declined opportunities out of the shared network.
- Membership is not consent to unsolicited campaigns. Every introduction request and outbound message remains explicitly human-approved; consent cannot transfer silently between desks.
- An expressed interest is not a committed budget, reserved slot or guaranteed partnership. CoHost stays read-only, and participation never depends on access to its internals.

~~~
Establish per-party consent, narrowly scoped publication and revocation rules before any cross-desk discovery. Match only intentionally shared information, preserve source freshness, and route handoffs through the approved introduction boundary rather than exposing private research records.
~~~
